# Mortal game design

Mortal begins at birth. Its journal groups major events, decisions, and actual stat changes by age. Each life begins in one of three fictional cities in Veyra. The character's household, natural talent, and other generated traits create different starting conditions.

## Phase 1 experience

- Random Life rolls a name, gender, hometown, household background, optional talent, attributes, and parents. Custom Life lets the player set the same profile fields and choose a birth year.
- The journal shows the character's age, city, education, balance, and four core attributes. Life Statistics shows the remaining attributes and background.
- Age Up advances one year. Early-life milestones introduce the player to choices. Optional events use context such as city, age, attributes, family circumstances, and talent. Some decisions return as consequences years later.
- The five bottom actions lead to Career, Relationships, Assets, and Activities, or advance the simulation directly. Phase 1 includes school progression and study, biographies and conversations for parents, a bank ledger, and a few activities.
- Completed lives retain their journal and a summary with age, cause of death, education, balance, and family.

The native visual system uses white and soft-blue surfaces, subtle dividers, pastel stat meters, and system emoji. Game copy starts in English. Common strings live in an English dictionary so more UI can move to translation keys as Bahasa Indonesia support is added.

## Phase 2.1 · Advanced education

- Primary, secondary, and high school track grades, attendance, and stress. Each stage has its own teacher and roster. The journal asks graduates whether to pursue university, vocational training, work, or a gap year.
- Alder Bay University, Bellmere University, and Northwick University offer nine undergraduate categories and three postgraduate courses. Veyra Technical College offers two vocational diplomas and adult high school equivalency. Program pages show the institution, duration, prerequisites, tuition, scholarship options, and skills.
- Players can seek admission, keep an offer for a limited time, enroll, and fund each academic year. A scholarship can cover tuition through graduation. Players short on funds can pause or withdraw and apply again later. Leaving school early opens the adult equivalency route once eligible.
- University cohorts have a professor and students with individual profiles. Players can befriend students and keep those friendships after school. Graduates can seek employment, and active NPCs can take suitable jobs.
- Academic Records shows completed, failed, and withdrawn study, plus current enrollment, grades, qualifications, and scholarships. Career shows the current education status and highest qualification alongside education links.

## Phase 2.2 · Career, employment, and personal economy

- Thirty-four roles across ten industries include entry jobs, vocational work, and degree-gated careers. Fictional employers operate in the cities of Veyra. Listings refresh yearly, and the player decides whether to accept an offer.
- Performance, stress, job security, satisfaction, reputation, industry experience, and employer culture shape promotions, raises, warnings, and dismissals. Players can choose a lighter or longer work week, resign, change employers, or retire from age 65.
- Each employer has a persistent supervisor, manager, and co-workers. Players can discuss tasks, spend time together, and make friends who stay accessible after leaving. Insults and pranks can harm a bond and damage standing at an active workplace.
- The ledger shows salary, tax, essentials, tuition, scholarships, and other spending. Unpaid essentials carry forward as arrears. Career History shows the real employment timeline and lifetime gross earnings.

## Phase 2.3 · Love, family, and independent NPC life

- An NPC retains one identity and portrait when a friendship, partnership, or family role changes. Traits affect social decisions, promotion prospects, ties with other NPCs, and friendship decay. Meeting someone adds an acquaintance. Shared history and regular contact can deepen a friendship.
- Romance requires two adults outside the family. Dating decisions depend on consent, attraction, compatibility and circumstances. Proposals can come from either partner. Weddings have three price tiers. Divorce settles a small cash amount and preserves both the former partner and the children’s family links.
- A sibling may be born during childhood, and a grandparent may be part of the tree. Adult spouses can mutually agree to welcome or adopt children through an abstract decision. Parenting supports learning, confidence, wellbeing, and the bond with the child.
- Active NPCs can finish education, work, save, retire, relocate, become ill, seek care, and form relationships. The simulation models selected contacts and a simplified household economy. It keeps player hiring, unpaid bills, property, and tuition in their existing systems.

## Phase 2.4 · Personal growth and lifestyle

- The player can develop 26 individual skills spanning sports, music, creative work, knowledge, and practical skills. My Skills shows six proficiency tiers, experience, recent progress, and achievements. Aptitude is discovered through experience and influences training efficiency.
- Sports and chess offer age-appropriate amateur competitions. Musicians can perform in a local recital, and creative skills open projects and showcases. Education and work develop relevant skills over time. Preferred skills can help a job application or review alongside the existing credentials.
- A practiced skill can become a hobby. Shared interests create conversation topics, improve compatibility, and open joint practice. Hobby groups can reintroduce known contacts. Children develop a small set of interests and can benefit from encouragement or optional paid lessons.
- Activities group repeatable wellbeing, entertainment, and travel alongside skill training, social activities, doctors, and appearance. Leisure affects existing health, happiness, energy, and stress. Results use the existing narrative pop-up and journal.

## Phase 3.0 · The Living World

- Veyra moves between expansion, boom, stagnation, recession, crisis, and recovery. The ten industries respond differently. Each city has its own hiring conditions, housing market, and public-service indicators.
- World reports show market conditions and optional fictional news. A small set of world events actively changes local or national indicators. Losing a job to redundancy produces a personal journal notice, while news about the wider economy stays in World.
- New offers and eligible raises or promotions reflect the market. Families experience changes through everyday spending and asset quotes. Adults can move to another city after resolving commitments and see how their new location changes opportunities and costs.
- Proficiency above 55 develops more gradually. Training variety and recent competitions or showcases reward applied practice. The effect of physical conditioning remains distinct from what the character knows about a skill.

## Phase 3.1 · Wealth, banking, and asset management

- The Assets menu shows net worth and liabilities alongside cash. Savings offer a predictable annual rate linked to Veyra’s economy. Creditworthiness gives adult borrowers a clear reason for a refusal and makes stronger applications eligible for better fixed rates.
- An owned house can become a residence or earn rent when occupied by a tenant. Location, upkeep, and vacancies shape the return. Maintenance, improvements, and vehicle repairs provide choices about when to spend cash to preserve usefulness or market value.
- Retirement offers an annual pension after enough contribution years. The Veyra index, technology, healthcare, and government bond funds let players take market risk through a small portfolio. The Life Summary records the final estate and major career, family, and skill milestones.
- Saved Lives supports backups and transfers between browser and native installs by JSON file. Each import creates a separate playable copy of the life.
